Best Beginner Disc Golf Discs

The biggest mistake new players make is buying a fast, powerful distance driver because it "goes far." It won't — not yet. For a beginner, the right discs are lighter and more understable, and you only need three of them to play great disc golf.

Why lighter and understable wins

Disc stability describes how a disc flies. Overstable discs fight against your throw and fade hard left (for a right-handed backhand); understable discs turn right and hold a straighter, longer line at slower arm speeds. Because beginners don't yet generate pro-level power, understable plastic actually flies farther and straighter for them. Lighter weights (150–165 g) are easier to throw and kinder to your shoulder while you learn form.

The only three discs you need

1. A putter. Used for putting and short, controlled approach shots. A stable, beadless putter is forgiving and flies predictably. This is the most-used disc in your bag — buy two or three of the same one so you can practice putting.

2. A midrange. Your straightest, most controllable disc for approaches and shorter drives. Pick a neutral-to-understable midrange — it will hold a straight line and is the easiest disc in the game to throw accurately.

3. A fairway driver (understable). Save distance drivers for later. An understable fairway driver gives you distance with control and teaches good form. Classic beginner-friendly options include understable fairways like the Innova Leopard, Latitude 64 Diamond, or Discraft Heat.

What weight should I throw?

Look for discs in the 150–165 gram range while you're learning. Lighter discs need less power to fly well and turn over more easily, which helps you feel the correct release. You can add heavier, more overstable discs as your arm speed grows.

What to avoid

  • Fast, overstable distance drivers (high-speed "22 mph" molds). They require power you don't have yet and will just fade hard and short.
  • Buying a huge starter bag of 10+ discs. More discs slow your progress. Learn to shape shots with three before adding more.
  • Premium plastic on day one. Cheaper base-plastic versions of the same molds fly the same and cost less while you're inevitably losing a few.

Master those three discs and you'll shoot better scores than someone carrying twenty they can't control. As your form develops, Disc Golf Go's Disc Selection lessons walk you through adding the right discs at the right time.
