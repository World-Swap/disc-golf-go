Putting

How to Putt in Disc Golf

Putting is where scores are won and lost. Most amateurs lose more strokes inside Circle 1 (33 feet / 10 meters) than anywhere else on the course. The good news: putting is the most trainable part of disc golf, and a few fundamentals will make an immediate difference.

1. The grip

There's no single "correct" putting grip, but the most common is the fan grip: spread your fingers across the underside of the disc for stability, with your thumb resting on top near the center. Keep it firm but relaxed — a tense, white-knuckle grip kills touch. Whatever grip you choose, use the exact same one on every putt.

2. Stance and balance

Stagger your feet with your throwing-side foot forward, pointed toward the basket, knees slightly bent. Your weight should feel balanced and centered so you can shift smoothly toward the target. Many players prefer a straddle stance in wind or on uneven lies for a more stable base — experiment and pick what feels repeatable.

3. The stroke

Power comes from your legs and core, not your arm. Load your weight slightly back, then push up and forward, releasing the disc on a smooth line toward the chains. Two styles work:

  • Push putt — minimal spin, the disc is "pushed" toward the basket on a flat line. Great for consistency in Circle 1.
  • Spin putt — more wrist snap and spin, which fights wind and reaches longer distances.

Beginners should start with a push-style stroke and add spin as they get comfortable. Follow through so your hand finishes pointing at the target.

4. Aim small

Don't aim at the whole basket — pick a single link of chain or the center pole and focus on it. Aiming small tightens your misses. On longer putts, aim for the top of the basket or just above the band so the disc drops into the chains rather than sailing over.

5. Build a repeatable routine

The best putters do the exact same thing every time: same number of practice reaches, same breath, same trigger. A consistent routine removes decision-making under pressure and is the single biggest key to putting well when it matters.

A simple practice drill

Take five putters and putt from 15 feet until you make 10 in a row. Then step back to 20, then 25, then 30 feet. This "putting ladder" builds both form and confidence. Fifteen focused minutes a few times a week will lower your scores faster than any new driver.
