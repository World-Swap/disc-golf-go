How it works

What the app actually does.

Tell it where you are

Four questions: what you can already do, what you want to get better at, where the strokes actually go, and whether you have an event booked. The library puts itself in order around the answers instead of starting everyone at lesson one.

▶

Watch & learn

Real coaching from disc golf's best. Every lesson plays the pro video right in the app, with the core tips and a guide to go deeper.

▲

Earn XP & missions

Complete lessons and the daily challenge to earn XP, rise through the skill tiers, and unlock the creator vault — every video your finished lessons have opened up, grouped by who taught it.

◎

Get ready for an event

Say you are signed up for one and you get a second plan, paced week by week and counting down to the date — what a tournament round asks that a casual one does not, and the rules you will be held to on a card.

The newest videos from 27 disc golf channels, pulled in every day — rounds, reviews, vlogs and coaching, all in one place, with the rest of each creator's uploads a tap away.

◷

It keeps you coming back

A tip each day, a streak that counts the days you turn up, and reminders when you stop — all of which you can switch off, because a training app you have muted is not training anybody.

Play

There's a game inside the app, and a tournament on top of it.

Throw Lab is a throwing game you play on your phone. Tap once to start the power bar, tap to lock it, tap to set your angle — then watch the fade bring the disc back, the way a real one does. No course, no GPS, no waiting for the weather. Every Monday a course is drawn at random and it becomes a tournament: the same eighteen holes for every player in the app.

  • Three discs, real flight numbersPutter, mid and driver, each thrown on its own speed, glide, turn and fade — overpower a disc and it turns over, just like it would in your hand.
  • 21 illustrated holesWoods, water, desert, canyon, winter. They're drawn from a shuffled bag, so a round never shows you the same hole twice.
  • Or play a real coursePick any of 1,500+ courses and the round is built from its actual holes and lengths — 3, 6, 9, or all 18.
  • Two shots at the weekAn attempt is spent the moment you tee off, so walking away from a bad start costs you one. Best of the two counts, and ties go to whoever posted first — there is a reason to play early.
  • Boards, and a record that can't driftRounds earn XP and drive daily, weekly, monthly and lifetime challenges. Training, Throw Lab and the tournament each have a leaderboard of their own, and every figure in your record — rounds, aces, birdies a round, average against par, wins, podiums — is counted from the rounds themselves.

FAQ

Disc golf questions, answered.

Is Disc Golf Go free?

Yes. Disc Golf Go is free to start on iOS, Android, and the web — all 134 training lessons and the pro coaching videos are included. Create an account to save your progress and XP.

How do I get better at disc golf?

Improvement comes from structured practice, not just more rounds. Disc Golf Go organizes the game into 13 skill paths — putting, driving, forehand, course strategy, mental game and more — with short video lessons from touring pros and the top coaching channels so you always know what to work on next.

Which pros are featured in the lessons?

Lessons draw on 62 creators — touring pros plus the channels that do the most disc golf teaching. Pros include Gannon Buhr, Scott Stokely, Paul McBeth, Ricky Wysocki, Simon Lizotte, Eagle McMahon, Kristin Tattar, Paige Pierce, Nate Sexton, Calvin Heimburg, Paul Ulibarri, Isaac Robinson, Chris Dickerson, Eric Oakley and Ezra Aderhold; channels include Dynamic Discs, Latitude 64, JustDisc, Overthrow Disc Golf, Robbie C, Gladiator and the PDGA.

What disc should a beginner throw?

Start with a putter and a lightweight, understable midrange or fairway driver. Lighter, understable plastic is easier to throw straight and flies farther for a new player than a fast, overstable distance driver. See our beginner disc guide for specifics.

Do I need to be at a course to use the app?

No. The training lessons work anywhere with no GPS required — practice in your yard or study on the couch, and play a Throw Lab round wherever you are. GPS is only used for optional course check-ins when you want to log a round at one of 1,500+ real courses.

What is Throw Lab?

Throw Lab is a disc golf game built into the app. You tap to set your power and your release angle, and the disc flies on its real flight numbers — fade and all. Play a quick 3, 6 or 9-hole round on one of 21 illustrated holes, or pick any of 1,500+ real courses and play its actual holes and lengths. Rounds earn XP, advance daily, weekly, monthly and lifetime challenges, and rank you on a Throw Lab leaderboard of its own.

Is there a tournament?

Yes — a new one every week. One course is drawn at random on Monday and it is the same 18 holes for every player in the app. You get two attempts, and an attempt is spent the moment you tee off, so leaving a bad round part-way through still uses one. The better of your two scores counts, and ties go to whoever posted first.

What is the Players Lounge?

A feed of the newest videos from 27 disc golf channels — tournament coverage, disc reviews, vlogs and coaching — refreshed every day. The ten most recent sit at the top and the rest are grouped by creator. Instructional videos are also filtered out into the training section, so lessons and entertainment stay separate.

How does earning XP work?

You earn XP by completing lessons and a rotating daily challenge. XP raises your skill tier and climbs the leaderboard, and finishing a lesson unlocks that pro's video in your Creators vault.
